II. The Designer Dilemma

xxx To start off, I think it’s self-evident that streetwear is my passion. It’s the cornerstone of my personal style, the aesthetic I gravitate toward, and the mode of expression in which I feel most at ease. Beyond its practicality, streetwear mirrors my general demeanor, embodying an effortless, yet deliberate, sense of sophistication.

That’s not to imply, however, that my fashion horizons are limited to streetwear alone. On the contrary, I’ve cultivated a deep appreciation for and knowledge of a variety of styles, even those that might be deemed antithetical to streetwear’s relaxed ethos. While I don’t often venture into these other stylistic realms personally, denying myself the richness of the broader fashion world would make life feel unbearably drab and uninspired. Fashion, at its core, is about inspiration, and inspiration knows no boundaries. We should always seek it, no matter the source.

For instance, my favorite brand in the world is Skoot, a Korean label that masterfully marries accessibility with avant-garde innovation. Skoot is the brainchild of two visionary designers, both of whom I hold in the highest regard. First, there’s Baek Ye-Jin, a Korean designer whose creations feel like dispatches from a distant future. Her work defies convention and resonates deeply with my own desire for the extraordinary. Then there’s Problemboy, a Canadian designer whose enigmatic persona only amplifies the allure of his craft. Specializing in graphic and print-focused designs, he brings a raw, compelling energy to Skoot that’s impossible to match. Together, these two creators form an unparalleled creative duo, and collaborating with them someday would be a dream come true.

Another designer I greatly admire is Oliver Sykes, a British creative force with a captivating presence—something I’ll elaborate on at a later entry (perhaps). His brand, Drop Dead, is an unapologetically edgy label steeped in darkwear aesthetics. What sets Drop Dead apart, beyond its moody and cohesive designs, is its incredible quality relative to its price point. Sykes approaches the brand as a labor of love, a passion project that eschews commercial trappings in favor of authenticity. The result is a collection that feels both personal and meticulously curated.

Lastly, I’d like to spotlight Max Reynders, a German designer and a trailblazer in the realm of modern techwear. His brand, MXDVS, represents a seamless fusion of contemporary European techwear with streetwear sensibilities. Reynders’ designs are edgy and, quite literally, dark, but they exude a conceptual depth that elevates them beyond mere aesthetics. He’s managed to carve out a unique space in fashion, creating pieces that are as functional as they are provocative. These are just a few examples of the designers and brands that fuel my fascination with fashion. They exemplify the kind of innovative thinking and boundary-pushing creativity that inspire me daily. While I could easily delve further into the intricacies of individual designers, for the sake of brevity, I’ll pivot the focus toward brands from now on. xxx

II. — 1.1 Variables and Domain

Let \(\mathcal{S}\) denote the set of all styles, and \(\mathcal{O}\) denote the set out of all outfits. The variables will represent elements in these sets:

  • \(x, y, z\dots \in \mathcal{S} \quad \text{(styles)}\)

  • \(o_1, o_2, o_3\dots \in \mathcal{O} \quad \text{(outfits)}\)

Thus, the domain of discourse is \(\mathcal{S} \cup \mathcal{O}\), i.e., the set of all styles and outfits.

III. Layers of Meaning

Disclaimer:

The following compilation represents a mere fraction of the vast and diverse world of fashion brands. While these selections offer a glimpse into various styles and designers, they are by no means exhaustive. Fashion is an ever-evolving landscape, rich with countless labels, each contributing uniquely to the tapestry of global style. I encourage you to embark on your own journey of discovery, exploring beyond this list to uncover brands that resonate with your personal aesthetic and values.

III. — 1. Anti-Aesthetic Reducibility

When it comes to brands, you’re already familiar with Pleasures, a label that blends accessibility with striking designs and inventive concepts. It’s a staple in graphic wear, combining bold aesthetics with a rebellious spirit. Brands like Very Rare, ABVHVHN, Jakischrist, MSCHF, lobobobo, XVESSEL, and Mementomori share this same vision—where graphic-driven fashion collides with urban pop sensibility, offering a visual language that’s both daring and expressive.

For those looking for a more refined, sober and grounded take, Unvain Studios merges minimalist elegance with subtle rebellion. Their clean lines and muted color palettes offer sophisticated streetwear with a quiet edge. Meanwhile, Mki Myuki Zoku offers a minimalist aesthetic with high-end tailoring, blending simplicity with luxury. Represent strikes a similar balance, providing modern utility and a streetwear sensibility with a polished, upscale approach.

Each of these brands speaks to a different facet of urban and high fashion, from graphic streetwear to refined tailoring. But this is just a glimpse into the wider fashion landscape. 1017 ALYX 9SM, Stüssy, and Naked Wolfe further enrich this scene, while brands like 404 and Polemique reflect the growing intersection of digital culture and fashion.

For deeper exploration, Haunted Starbucks provides a satirical take on the omnipresence of big brands.

Sites like ProjectISR curate emerging Chinese designers, offering a window into an exciting new scene. Form Space, curated by the visionary michael angelo ⠍⠊⠉⠓⠁⠑⠇⠁⠝⠑⠛⠇⠕, offers avant-garde pieces that push fashion’s boundaries.

III. — 2. Compactly Stochastic Invariable Dynamics

We will now explore some brands that are somewhat harder to access—whether due to their archival status or their price tags. Finding pieces from these brands often requires diligent “crate digging” on platforms like Grailed, Depop, Farfetch, SSENSE, Yoox, or physically by hunting through thrift stores all the way to high-end galleries. My personal favorite gallery is Vitkac in Warsaw. Which are all also extremely potent tools to discover new brands and designers. These pieces can be expensive, but if you know how to search, you can find them at affordable prices.

Here’s a curated selection of only a handful of these brands in no particular order.

  • Vetements: Pioneers of high-concept streetwear, Vetements is notorious for pushing boundaries. Known for their deconstructed designs, subversive takes on everyday items, and collaboration-heavy collections, finding authentic Vetements pieces can be challenging, but the brand’s radical aesthetic has made them a standout in fashion.

  • Gallery Dept.: Fusing art and fashion, Gallery Dept. brings an artistic, grunge-inspired approach to streetwear. The brand’s pieces are often hand-painted and distressed, offering a blend of vintage and high-fashion sensibilities. It’s a brand that’s equal parts street culture and fine art.

  • Lanvin: While not universally loved, Lanvin has undeniably crafted some legendary pieces, especially in the footwear department. The Curb Sneakers are a perfect example of their exploration of chunky, bold footwear. For those who appreciate refined yet contemporary designs, Lanvin offers a wealth of surprising treasures.

  • Diesel: stands out as a long-time mainstay in the fashion world, offering rugged yet refined designs. Known for its denim heritage, the brand consistently mixes industrial aesthetics with luxurious touches. While grounded, Diesel brings bold concepts to everyday wear, offering quality and style that endures.

“I remember it as vividly as if it were yesterday, though I must have been no more than 10 or 11 wandering through Jockey Plaza’s shiny new boulevard with my mom when it caught my eye—a Diesel t-shirt, front and center in the window of their just-opened store. Back then, I didn’t know the first thing about high fashion or personal style. But the moment I saw that shirt, I knew I needed it. It wasn’t just a t-shirt—it was the t-shirt. My mom, bless her, made it happen, and just like that, I had the first piece of high fashion that I chose willingly. It was the start of something I didn’t even realize was beginning.”

  • Alexander Wang: Innovative and boundary-pushing, Alexander Wang is all about redefining modern fashion. Wang’s designs often blur the lines between street style and high fashion, and his ability to mix dark, gritty vibes with polished elegance makes his collections both intriguing and highly wearable.

  • Rough Simmons (Not to be confused with Raf Simmons): The name says it all—Rough Simmons is about raw, in-your-face fashion with a heavily avant-garde approach. Their pieces exude a sense of rebellion and boldness that challenges conventional aesthetics. It’s one for those who want fashion that makes a statement, both in its materials and design.

  • Raf Simmons: Raf Simons is renowned for blending youth culture with minimalist sophistication. His collections often feature clean lines, thought-provoking graphics, and an avant-garde sensibility that redefines contemporary fashion. Simons’ work resonates deeply with those who appreciate intellectual design paired with wearable art.

  • Rick Owens: Rick Owens is known for his conceptual and minimalist approach, creating designs that challenge traditional notions of beauty and form. Whether it’s oversized silhouettes or futuristic footwear, Owens constantly plays with proportions, making his work stand out in a way few other designers can.

  • Kiko Kostadinov: Kiko Kostadinov stands at the forefront of the next generation of designers, mixing technical innovation with artistic vision. Known for his experimental shapes and use of unconventional fabrics, Kostadinov’s collections blur the line between fashion and architecture, offering garments that look like they were pulled from the future.

  • Heron Preston: A key player in the streetwear scene, Heron Preston combines high-fashion concepts with the irreverence of urban culture. His collections often incorporate bold graphics and logo-heavy pieces, pushing the boundaries of casual fashion into something more elevated.

  • Balenciaga: Balenciaga is a polarizing brand. While their pieces may not always be universally appealing, they certainly leave an impression. Known for transforming everyday items into high-fashion statements, Balenciaga has redefined luxury streetwear, especially with their oversized silhouettes and provocative designs. Specifically the designs by Demna.

  • Heliot Emil: Known for their futuristic designs, Heliot Emil offers an innovative approach to streetwear. The brand’s distinctively dark and architectural style is often paired with technical fabrics, producing a sleek and progressive aesthetic. Their balaclavas and masks, like the one I personally use, are an iconic part of their design repertoire.

  • Made by Revenge: Made by Revenge is a Russian brand known for its understated coolness and subversive ideas. It’s a brand that plays with cultural and aesthetic themes, creating pieces that speak to those who want something unconventional but still wearable.

  • Fear of God (NOT ESSENTIALS): A luxury brand that marries street style with high-end materials, Fear of God is defined by its minimalistic designs and oversized silhouettes. Its emphasis on timeless pieces that fit within the streetwear aesthetic has earned it a loyal following.

  • 1989 Studio: 1989 Studio is all about creative, out-of-the-box thinking. The brand brings bold, unconventional ideas to life, mixing elements of street culture with high-fashion influences, resulting in visually striking designs that cater to those who want to stand out.

  • Palm Angels: A highly recognizable name in luxury streetwear, Palm Angels blends skate culture with upscale design. Their collections often feature iconic graphics and bold logos, but what truly sets them apart is their ability to infuse a sense of individuality and cool factor into each piece.

  • Moncler: While known for their outerwear, Moncler brings a high-fashion touch to performance wear. Their collaborations with designers like Rick Owens and Giambattista Valli have solidified their place in the luxury fashion scene, but their mainstream appeal is undeniable.

  • Burberry: While not really my personal style, Burberry remains a staple of British luxury. Known for its iconic trench coats and checkered patterns, the brand’s newer collections often feature a mix of classic design with modern twists, keeping it relevant in today’s fashion world.

  • Carhartt WIP: Carhartt WIP takes the workwear brand Carhartt and transforms it for a more fashion-forward audience. The brand combines durability with streetwear sensibilities, making it an essential for those who appreciate functional, yet stylish, clothing. Carhartt on steroids (or crack) if you may.

  • Junya Watanabe: Known for his conceptual approach, Junya Watanabe takes traditional fashion and flips it on its head. His collections often feature experimental fabrics and unusual cuts, offering an avant-garde take on high fashion that appeals to the most daring fashion-forward individuals.

  • off-White: Off-White has been through ups and downs, especially after the passing of its founder Virgil Abloh, but it still holds a place in the fashion world. The brand blends luxury with streetwear, featuring graphic-heavy designs and distinctive motifs that have captured the public’s imagination.

  • Enfants Riches Deprimes: Ultra-stylized, Enfants Riches Deprimes brings a unique vision of luxury streetwear to life. Often adorned with luxury fabrications, the brand is all about blending opulence with rebellion, making it one of the most coveted high-end streetwear brands out there.

  • Acne Studios: Known for its eclectic and sometimes offbeat designs, Acne Studios brings a high-fashion edge to casual wear. Their collections often mix understated minimalism with bold graphic elements, creating a unique juxtaposition that appeals to lovers of avant-garde fashion.

  • Mostly Heard Rarely Seen:- A brand that blends intricate craftsmanship with streetwear sensibilities. Their use of unique textures, clever detailing, and a play on visuals gives their pieces a distinct identity. Their designs challenge norms while staying wearable, creating a perfect balance between conceptual and practical.

Once again, this is just a tiny glimpse into the immense world of brands out there. Use this as a starting point to dive deeper, explore, and discover the vast creativity and innovation that the fashion world has to offer.

III. — 3. “7-7-7:30” An Essay on Recursive Computable Decomposition

The concept of 1-of-1s is such a pivotal part of fashion—a space where individuality and artistry collide to create truly unique pieces. So, let me give this its well-deserved spotlight and share some examples of brands that are really making waves in this area.

  • OVERDXSE: This brand is the epitome of edge. Their designs don’t just push boundaries; they leap over them with an unapologetic attitude that feels authentic and raw. There’s a soulfulness to their work, a balance between grit and creativity that’s very hard to match.

  • READYMADE: Known for repurposing military fabrics and blending them with modern silhouettes, READYMADE is a masterclass in craftsmanship. Each piece feels deliberate, carrying an energy that speaks to its one-of-a-kind nature.

  • R13: This brand’s approach is as rebellious as it is refined. While their 1-of-1 offerings are more prominent in their women’s collections, their aesthetic is universally intriguing. Deconstructed denim, oversized silhouettes, and that grunge-meets-luxury vibe make them stand out.

  • Can’t Look/Look At Yourself: This is a young designer who channels heavy inspiration from Problemboy, but with a fresh spin. Their graphic wear feels strikingly mature for someone just starting out, blending bold visuals with a sense of experimentation. They’re definitely one to watch.

  • Nika Wear: Another rising star in the 1-of-1 space, Nika Wear combines unique ideas with impressive execution. There’s a sense of ambition in their work that’s impossible to overlook, and it’s exciting to see how they’re carving out a place in the scene.

Ok, now let’s address the elephant in the room: Antisocial Social Club. Sure, technically, their pieces fall into the 1-of-1 category, but let’s call it what it is—trash disguised as exclusivity. The fabric quality is abysmal, the designs are uninspired, and there’s no dedicated designer driving the brand’s vision. Their business model is even worse, relying on artificial scarcity to inflate prices and create a false sense of demand. It’s less about artistry and more about playing the resale game, which, frankly, cheapens the entire concept of 1-of-1s.
